## One-sentence overview
Bint Saeed’s heritage hub documents the rich cultural heritage of the United Arab Emirates, Emirati crafts Al Talli, Al Khous, and Sadu, plus the battoulah (gold burqa mask) of Gulf women’s dress, from a luxury abaya house in Abu Dhabi.

## Core crafts
### Al Talli (التلي)
Traditional Emirati decorative craft of hand-worked metallic and coloured thread bands, historically used to adorn women’s clothing in the UAE. Inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2022. Living practice at the House of Artisans, Abu Dhabi.
Deep page: https://www.bintsaeed.com/heritage/al-talli
Journal essay: https://substack.com/home/post/p-212370383 (The Golden Thread Between Women)
UNESCO: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/al-talli-embroidery-tradition-01985

### Al Khous (الخوص)
Emirati palm-frond weaving from the date palm, mats, baskets, fans, and structural forms rooted in oasis life across the United Arab Emirates.
Chapter: https://www.bintsaeed.com/heritage/khous

### Sadu (السدو / Al Sadu)
Bedouin weaving tradition of the Arabian Peninsula, including the UAE. Geometric motifs carry desert meaning. First inscribed for urgent safeguarding in 2011; transferred in 2025 to UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
Chapter: https://www.bintsaeed.com/heritage/sadu
UNESCO: https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/al-sadu-traditional-weaving-skills-in-the-united-arab-emirates-02223

### Battoulah / Batula (البرقع الذهبي)
Traditional Gulf metal face mask, often gold-, brass-, or silver-toned, historically associated with women’s dress in parts of the UAE and neighbouring Gulf societies. In English often called a gold burqa mask or Emirati gold mask; distinct from the fabric Afghan burqa. Cultural heritage context only, not a Bint Saeed product.

## How Bint Saeed relates to this heritage
Bint Saeed is an Abu Dhabi fashion house that studies Emirati craft languages, metallic line (Al Talli), palm structure (Al Khous), desert geometry (Sadu), and translates those observations into contemporary abayas. The house does not recreate traditional dress as costume; it carries heritage forward through design.
